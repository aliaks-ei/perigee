## Evidence and interpretation

The fixed visual baseline is [`sirius-portrait-v1.png`](../public/assets/objects/sirius-portrait-v1.png). It is approved artwork, not a resolved photospheric image. Its limb, halo, and background stars remain on a flat portrait layer in production. The new 2048 × 1024 [`sirius-granulation-review-v1.webp`](../public/assets/objects/sirius-granulation-review-v1.webp) is **original synthetic review art** generated by [`generate-sirius-texture.mjs`](../scripts/hybrid-review/generate-sirius-texture.mjs) from continuous three-dimensional noise. The script writes an intermediate PPM; `magick tmp/hybrid-h6-sirius/sirius-surface.ppm -define webp:lossless=true -quality 100 public/assets/objects/sirius-granulation-review-v1.webp` produces the review asset without a lossy longitude seam. No portrait or telescope pixels, baked limb, halo, diffraction pattern, stars, or Sirius B imagery enter the map. Its cell sizes and colors are visual reconstruction choices, not measured surface scales or a global map.

The A-star simulations justify restrained artistic granulation only. None of these sources supplies a measured global Sirius A surface.

## Renderer and motion decision

The production policy now selects the emissive sphere; `RenderingReview.sirius = 'portrait'` retains an explicit rollback. The globe shares the stable group → pole → spin hierarchy, texture lease and cancellation cleanup, existing quality selection, point-star transition, `CelestialClock`, hidden-tab/reduced-motion suspension, and frozen still-export path. The halo is a separate view-facing optical layer so it never wraps around or rotates with the surface. No planet lighting or Sun-facing terminator is used. The review `longitudeDegrees` is a deterministic turntable offset. Bulk spin remains disabled (`periodSeconds: null`); the shader's low-amplitude surface evolution uses the independent evolution clock.

The map is decoded as sRGB color. The approved live composer applies bloom and AgX before the display-referred portrait layer. The globe and optical halo are rendered on that same post-tone-map layer with `toneMapped: false`, preserving authored pale blue-white color and avoiding double tone mapping. `StillRenderer` explicitly applies AgX then the portrait layer to its display target before sRGB output, matching the live order. The globe's core is capped below hard white, its limb is luminous without a baked edge, and its optical halo is deliberately narrow.
